# SJ Design Philosophy

SJ's design revolves around movement — helping people reach their destinations, move forward in digital flows, and be part of the sustainable travel movement.

## Core Philosophy

1. **We design to set people in motion** — SJ guides customers throughout their entire journey: before, during, and after. Help people make decisions, choose correctly, and move forward.

2. **We design for people on the move** — Most users are on mobile phones in busy environments. Always start with small screens and consider real-world usage contexts.

3. **We design to keep moving** — Actively meet current and future customer needs. Test, question, and reflect continuously.

4. **We design to connect** — Understand customers from their situation. Be simple, reliable, human, and delightful through actions and products.

5. **We design because we're part of an important movement** — Design should feel relevant, sustainable, and inclusive. SJ is for everyone, now and in the future.

## Design Principles

These principles guide design decisions:

### 1. Think in flows
A customer journey is complex — more than isolated steps or features. See the whole picture and understand how different parts create value together.

### 2. Let needs drive technology
Technology should solve real customer problems, not become a barrier. Every user should feel like a super user regardless of technical background.

### 3. Customer first, conversion second
Put the customer's needs first. Never trick anyone into making a choice they don't want. Honesty and transparency create long-term satisfied customers.

### 4. The goal is the goal
Digital services are tools to help customers reach their own goals — not end goals in themselves. Always ask: "What is the actual goal?"

### 5. Hurry slowly
Simplicity and clarity outweigh a minimal number of steps. Evaluate the overall experience, not the number of pages or actions.

### 6. Be empathetic
A calm customer differs from a stressed one. Adapt design to the customer's state of mind through tempo, tone, and complexity.

### 7. Write for humans
Text and image should tell the story in the most effective way. Avoid bureaucratic complexity. Write for people — it should be noticeable.

### 8. Don't be annoying
Communication and solutions should be relevant and sparse. Sometimes the best customer experience is simply being left alone.

### 9. You are not the customer
Design based on actual, varying customer needs — not your own perspective. Accessibility is a given. The goal is to be perceived as caring and reliable.
